welcome to my little corner :)

Hi! You've somehow found my personal KIR journal. This is where I write down random experiences, thoughts, experiments, stories, and things I probably would've forgotten otherwise.

KIR has become one of those places where I can actually try things, make mistakes, learn something, and occasionally wonder why my experiment decided not to cooperate.

So yeah... welcome!

Welcome to my KIR Journal!
posted: September 2026

I decided to make this little website because there are way too many things happening during KIR that are actually worth remembering.

Not necessarily huge achievements. Sometimes it's just a random discussion, an experiment that failed, an idea that suddenly appeared, or a really long afternoon trying to figure something out.

"Sometimes the most useful part of research is finding out that your first idea was wrong."

That's basically what this website is about.

— me, probably writing this instead of doing the actual experiment

Why I Joined KIR in the First Place
posted: September 2026

Honestly, I don't think I fully understood what KIR would be like when I first got involved.

At first, the word "research" sounded like something extremely serious. Papers. Data. Experiments. Complicated words.

But after actually participating, I realized that research can also start from something really simple:

  • "Why does this happen?"
  • "Can this be improved?"
  • "What if I tried this?"
  • "Okay... why did that fail?"

And somehow, those questions can turn into an actual project.

The Experiment That Didn't Go According to Plan
posted: August 2026

This is probably one of the most important things KIR has taught me.

An experiment doesn't always work.

Sometimes the result is weird. Sometimes the material doesn't behave the way you expected. Sometimes the numbers don't make sense.

And sometimes you stare at the result for five minutes thinking:

"...okay, what happened here?"

But that's also part of the process.

Instead of immediately calling it a failure, you can ask why it happened. Maybe something was wrong with the method. Maybe the variables weren't controlled. Maybe the original idea needs to change.

And suddenly, the failed experiment becomes another thing to learn from.

Things KIR Has Taught Me So Far
posted: July 2026

If I had to make a small list of things I've learned from KIR, it would probably look like this:

  1. Don't assume your first idea is correct.
  2. Write things down.
  3. Data is more useful than guessing.
  4. A failed experiment can still teach you something.
  5. Research takes patience.
  6. Working with other people changes your idea.
  7. Sometimes you need to start over.

And probably the most important one:

Don't be afraid to ask "why?"
